The Actor-Critic Model

How one prediction error teaches an agent what to expect and what to do · about 5½ minutes, narrated, with captions

What you'll see

A rat learns a two-lever task: after a light turns on, the orange lever gives juice and the blue lever gives nothing. The video follows information through the actor-critic network on each trial and then maps each part onto the brain.

  1. The parts of the model: environment, critic, actor, and the prediction error δ
  2. Three quantities: the critic's prediction V, the actor's policy, and δ = r + V(new) − V(old)
  3. Trial 1 step by step: an unexpected reward produces a positive δ
  4. Trial 2: exploring the blue lever produces a negative δ
  5. Fast-forward: the prediction error moves from the reward to the cue
  6. After learning, and a probe trial in which the reward is withheld
  7. The link to dopamine recordings (Schultz, Dayan & Montague, 1997)
  8. Mapping onto the brain: frontal cortex, ventral and dorsolateral striatum, VTA/SNc, dopamine

Model details

The critic learns with a rate of 0.5, so each update moves V halfway toward the outcome. The actor picks between the levers with a softmax, and δ adjusts the preference for the lever it just pressed. The numbers in the fast-forward come from an actual simulation of this model.
